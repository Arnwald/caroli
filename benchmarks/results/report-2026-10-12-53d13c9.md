# Transport benchmarks, 2026-10-12 (second campaign)

caroli against Kwant 1.5 (MUMPS, on Accelerate), TBtrans (SIESTA 5.4.2) and Quantica.jl 1.4 on
the problems of `benchmarks/transport.toml`, on the idle Mac mini M4 Pro (10 performance and 4
efficiency cores). Every code solves the same exported Hamiltonian. "max ΔT" is the largest
difference of a code's transmissions from caroli's. caroli runs at commit 53d13c9; Kwant,
TBtrans and Quantica.jl do not depend on it.

## Summary

- **The fastest other code.** Kwant is the fastest of the three from W = 64 up, except on the
  long strips up to W = 64, where Quantica.jl is ahead. At W = 32, TBtrans (square lattice) and
  Quantica.jl (graphene) beat Kwant. TBtrans's block-tridiagonal solver costs O(L W³): it falls
  behind on square samples (6.9 s per energy at W = 256, Kwant 1.07 s). Quantica.jl's sparse LU
  and lead solver are 1.5–7× slower than Kwant on large samples, and its BHZ leads are slow
  (207 s per energy at W = 256).
- **One thread, per energy, against the fastest other code in each case:** caroli is 11–17×
  faster on the square lattice (W up to 512), 14–20× on the four-terminal cross, 6–12× on the
  long strips, 4.7–9× on BHZ and 4.6–7.3× on graphene. At the largest sizes it needs 3–4.5× less
  memory than Kwant (1.1× on the cross).
- **All the cores** (56 energies, builds included): caroli on 14 threads against Kwant on 14
  processes is 10× faster on the largest square samples, the strips, the cross and BHZ, and
  4.7–5.9× on graphene. At small sizes the start-up of the Kwant processes dominates (up to
  100×): read those rows as latency, not throughput.
- **One energy on all the cores**: caroli's nested dissection runs its branches in parallel: on
  14 threads one energy takes 2× less time than on one at W = 512, and 5× less on the strips.
  Kwant (MUMPS) is sequential.
- **Agreement**: Kwant to 1e-10 (2e-7 for BHZ at W = 256); TBtrans to about 1e-5, from its
  electrode broadening η = 1e-6 eV; Quantica.jl to 1e-4–3e-3, from the √ε broadening it adds to
  real energies and its lead solver.
- **Since the first campaign** (report-2026-10-12-08246f7): caroli's single-thread times went
  down 1.6–1.7× on the square lattice, 1.8× on the cross, 2× on graphene, 1.15× on the strips and
  1.1× on BHZ (products and lead spectra through Accelerate, assembly plans, small-front
  elimination, real pencil for real leads). On 14 threads, large samples scale 4–6× over one thread: the dense kernels share the
  matrix units and the memory bandwidth of each cluster, and running fewer energies at a time
  with more threads each is slower.

## Settings

- Kwant: MUMPS with its default METIS ordering (other orderings and `nrhs` gave nothing), only
  the needed block of the scattering matrix, no Hermiticity check, BLAS from Accelerate
  (5–10 % faster than OpenBLAS). On all the cores: a pool of 14 processes.
- TBtrans: electrodes at η = 1e-6 eV (agreement about 1e-5; 20 % more time than the default
  1 meV), double-precision output, per-energy time from two runs. Real Hamiltonians only (no
  BHZ) and two terminals; up to W = 256.
- Quantica.jl: GS.SparseLU for the sample, GS.Schur for the leads, a small instance first so
  that compilation is not timed. Two terminals, up to W = 256. Its memory includes the Julia
  runtime (1.1 GB).
- caroli: nested dissection (`NestedTwoTerminal`, `NestedMultiTerminal`), independent blocks
  solved separately (the two spin blocks of BHZ).

Machine: Mac-mini-de-Arnaud (Apple M4 Pro). caroli commits: 1807a24, 53d13c9, 69a6eff.

- Kwant 1.5.0 (kwant.solvers.mumps, environment kwant-accelerate)
- TBtrans SIESTA 5.4.2 (TBtrans (BTD), electrodes at eta = 1e-06 eV)
- Quantica.jl 1.4.0 (GS.SparseLU, GS.Schur leads)
